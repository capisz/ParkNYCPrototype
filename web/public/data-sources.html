NYC Parking Planner

Data sources and limitations

Parking

  • NYC DOT Parking Regulation Locations and Signs — checked daily; unavailable for classifications after 48 hours without a source update.
  • NYC DOT Parking Meters — metered blockface context; unavailable after 62 days.
  • NYC DOT Alternate Side Parking Suspension Calendar — the reviewed 2026 schedule modifies only explicitly recognized alternate-side, Sunday, meter, and major-holiday rules. Emergency changes still require NYC 311 verification.
  • NYC DCWP Issued Licenses — filtered to active Garage & Parking Lot licenses; unavailable after 14 days.
  • NYC DEP Citywide Hydrants — authoritative point inventory with approximate 15-foot visibility rings.

Street Pavement Ratings centerlines are not approved curb-side geometry and stay hidden from the public viewport. Separately, a fresh official ParkNYC meter blockface may appear yellow when its all-vehicle payment schedule covers the full interval, red for a fresh supported high-confidence prohibition, or reference-green only when current linked sign records and a recognized meter schedule fully resolve the interval with no active payment or recognized prohibition. Unresolved regulatory signs remain gray. These informational lines are non-recommendable and do not prove exact legal curb extent or physical availability. Hydrant rings are informational radial buffers, not curb-linked legal extents; always verify every posted sign, the physical hydrant, and the curb.

Destination search

Live address results use the NYC Planning Labs geocoder. If that provider is unavailable, a small reviewed fallback directory can resolve only named landmarks and clearly labeled neighborhood centers. Neighborhood-center results are approximate starting points, not street addresses.

Transit

Planned sources include MTA GTFS schedules, GTFS-Realtime updates and alerts, Bus Time, and current elevator and escalator outages. Park-and-ride stays disabled until routing, feed freshness, transfers, and accessibility pass the pilot validation corpus.

Color meanings

Red means cannot park. Yellow means payment is required. Green means likely legally free for the selected interval—verify posted signs. Gray means the evidence is incomplete—check signs.

The colors describe assumed legal curb status, not physical vacancy. Pidge does not know whether another vehicle occupies a space.
